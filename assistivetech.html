Assistive Technology CAD Prototype

My APSC 100 group and I created an assistive technology CAD prototype. We developed the Spin & Squeeze, a device designed to empower those with limited motor skills and mobility to operate a tube of toothpaste independently. We created a promotional sheet to share our concept and an elevator pitch to go along with it.

My role in my team was to create our elevator pitch that focused on the problem, solution, and its relevance. This meant conveying the necessity and urgency that our prototype be developed to help those with limited motor control.

This project helped me realize why I want to go into engineering. As an engineer, you have the ability to create prototypes and devices that can help people. Even small changes can make a tremendous impact on someone’s life. It is this knowledge that I continue to keep in the back of my mind whenever I am working on a project or my studies to keep me motivated to become a P. Eng to help those around me.

Script:

Paralyzed individuals are no longer able to perform everyday activities that were once second nature, just like our client Ken Fraser. Daily tasks such as squeezing toothpaste from the tube become nearly impossible to perform.

Our patentable assistive device, the Spin & Squeeze, consists of two hollow cylinders that rotate with a gear mechanism to squeeze toothpaste from the tube. The 6mm radius handle fits into the ring and pinkie fingers, taking advantage of muscle tightness in that area. Finally, the included cap remover further improves independence.

Our product makes the unimaginable transition to relying on assistive technology as seamless as possible. Your investment in our simple and intuitive design will help individuals regain their independence and confidence post-paralysis.
